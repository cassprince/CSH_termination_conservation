### Translation termination factors: their evolution, regulation, and versatility

## Authors: Cassidy R. Prince, Heather A. Feaga

# Abstract:
The genetic code is nearly universal, with each codon being recognized by a tRNA that delivers the correct amino acid to the ribosome to produce a polypeptide. The stop codons UAA, UGA, and UAG signal the end of the polypeptide sequence since they have no cognate tRNA to decode them. Instead, a protein release factor recognizes the stop codon and coordinates hydrolysis of the bond linking the peptide to the final tRNA. Peptide release allows for subsequent ribosome recycling. While the three stop codons are nearly universal in all species on the planet, the termination factors that recognize them arose independently in bacteria and archaea, having an unrelated protein sequence but arriving at a convergent function. Here, we review the evolution of translation termination, mechanisms used to regulate release factor expression, and the versatile function of termination factors in mediating ribosome quality control.
